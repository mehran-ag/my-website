Machine Learning

I have applied machine learning techniques in several research projects. In two published journal articles, I used Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural networks to predict tumour growth under different conditions. This approach reduced the number of simulations required to investigate tumour behaviour while allowing us to explore a wider range of scenarios. In another project, I used a Generalized Regression Neural Network (GRNN) to investigate the effects of different doses of the anti-angiogenic drug Endostatin. This enabled us to extend our predictions beyond the time frames covered by the original simulations and explore tumour behaviour over longer periods following treatment.

In another project, I used machine learning to develop a K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN) model to distinguish between COVID-19 and influenza based on patients' reported symptoms before they visited a general practitioner (GP). The program asks patients relevant questions and uses their responses to classify cases into COVID-19 or influenza groups for further assessment.

Computational simulation

As a mechanical engineer, I have worked primarily with two-dimensional and three-dimensional computational simulations. Mesh generation is one of the most interesting and important aspects of these simulations, as mesh quality can significantly affect the accuracy and reliability of the results. Therefore, the ability to generate appropriate structured and unstructured meshes is an essential skill in computational modelling.

I have developed codes for mesh generation, computational discretisation, and the simulation of mechanical phenomena, including fluid flow and heat transfer. Additionally, I have used commercial tools such as Gambit, ANSYS Meshing, and COMSOL's built-in meshing tools to generate meshes, as well as ANSYS Fluent and COMSOL to simulate these phenomena.
